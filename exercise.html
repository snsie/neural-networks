Pick a question worth exploring

Start with one of these ideas or propose your own. Each idea links to a free dataset that needs no sign-in. If you propose your own idea, use public or synthetic data. Credit your data source on your site.

Wearable Activity Detective

Can a neural network tell walking from stair climbing? Let visitors guess from an accelerometer trace, reveal the prediction, and explore a mistake.

Data: UCI Human Activity Recognition Using Smartphones. It has 30 people and six labeled activities, including walking, walking upstairs and walking downstairs. It comes already split into train and test sets. Use the 561 ready-made features for the model. Use the raw signals in Inertial Signals for the traces visitors see. License: CC BY 4.0.

Clinic Wait-Time Simulator

How do staffing and queue size affect wait times? Let visitors adjust a simulated clinic and see how the predicted wait changes.

Data: clinic-wait-times.csv. It has 2,000 simulated visits with hour, weekday, staff on shift, queue length and wait in minutes. We made it up: waits grow with the queue, shrink with more staff, and run longer at peak hours and on Mondays.

Readmission Threshold Lab

What happens when you flag more discharges for follow-up? Let visitors move a threshold slider and see how many readmissions the model catches or misses.

Data: UCI Diabetes 130-US Hospitals, 1999–2008. It has about 100,000 hospital stays. Predict readmitted = “<30” (about 11% of stays). Start with the eight numeric columns, such as days in hospital and prior visits. Split by patient_nbr so no patient appears in both train and test. Expect a modest model. The lesson is in the threshold trade-off.

Build your exhibit

  1. Train and evaluate a small neural network

    Download the dataset linked on your idea card, or bring your own. Define what the model takes in and what it predicts. Keep a test set separate from training and tuning. Compare the network with a simple baseline on that test set, such as the most frequent activity or the median training wait time. Pick one mistake and explain what might have helped.

  2. Design one meaningful interaction

    Give visitors a way to reveal, filter, or compare predictions. You can export held-out predictions for a static gallery. If a visitor enters new inputs, run the trained model on those inputs. Aim for one clear discovery the visitor can make.

  3. Add a WebGPU chatbot

    Use a small pretrained language model with WebLLM to run chat inference in the browser. Write a short guide with the site’s purpose, data, model inputs, test results, and limitations. Pass that guide to the chatbot as context and ask it to say when the guide lacks an answer.

    Check WebGPU on the device you’ll demo with. Let visitors choose Enable chat before loading the model, show loading progress, and keep the exhibit usable if chat can’t load.

    Three questions to test your chatbot
    • Data: Where did your data come from?
    • Results: How did your model compare with the baseline?
    • Outside the guide: How well did this model work on a dataset you never tested?

    Check each answer against your site and revise any unsupported claims.

Split the work, then review together

Model and evaluation lead

Prepare the data, train the neural network, and compare it with the baseline. During review, test the chatbot’s answers against your results.

Website and chatbot lead

Build the visitor interaction and integrate the chat widget. During review, walk your partner through the highlighted model mistake.

Write the site guide together. Both partners should be ready to explain a prediction and demonstrate how they checked a chatbot answer.
