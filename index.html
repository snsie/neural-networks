Mean squared error vs. binary cross-entropy

Both losses score how far a prediction is from the target. For binary classification, y is the true label (0 or 1) and p is the predicted probability of class 1. The training loss averages the per-example losses.

Mean squared error (MSE)

(p − y)2

Squares the prediction error. MSE is commonly used for regression, where the target is a number. With binary labels and probabilities, each loss is between 0 and 1.

Binary cross-entropy (BCE)

−y ln(p) − (1 − y) ln(1 − p)

Takes the negative natural log of the probability assigned to the true class. As that probability approaches zero, the loss grows without bound.

Both plots show the loss when the true label is 1. They use the same scale, and BCE continues above the plot as the predicted probability approaches zero.
